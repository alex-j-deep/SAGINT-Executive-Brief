## Summary

I met with [[Jacob Clayton]], founder and CEO of [[SAGINT]], after an introduction from [[Bryan Fenton]]. The conversation centered on SAGINT's effort to build what Jacob described as an **economic warfare operating system** for critical minerals and other strategically important supply chains.

The core idea is that the United States and its allies are not competing effectively at the **market infrastructure level**. Jacob argued that China has significant leverage over critical minerals not only through physical supply chains, but through pricing, exchange infrastructure, information control, and the lack of trusted traceability. SAGINT is trying to build a non-custodial traceability and digital asset infrastructure that can make critical minerals compliant, bankable, insurable, and usable by financial institutions, insurers, government agencies, and industry.

The conversation moved from an exploratory introduction into a more concrete discussion about a possible senior operating role for me. Jacob specifically said that I seemed like a strong fit for a **Chief of Staff** or possibly **Chief Strategy** type role focused on building the organization, directing startup energy, creating operating discipline, and helping build the right executive and technical team after the company's expected funding event.

## Context

GEN Fenton introduced me to Jacob as part of SAGINT's search for senior talent. Jacob said he had asked GEN Fenton for his "all-star team" and that Fenton made key introductions. Jacob also said he would like GEN Fenton to eventually join the board after any required cooling off period.

Jacob framed SAGINT's work as similar in spirit to the economic warfare ideas GEN Fenton has been advancing.

## Jacob's Core Thesis

Jacob's core argument is that rare earths and critical minerals are not just a supply chain problem. They are a market infrastructure and economic warfare problem.

Key points from Jacob:

- China controls critical minerals through pricing, infrastructure, information, and supply chain opacity.
- The London Metals Exchange helps set prices for many critical minerals, and Jacob argued that China can manipulate this because of Hong Kong's ownership role.
- The United States is trying to secure critical minerals but is not competing effectively at the market infrastructure level.
- The U.S. lacks a targeting system for economic warfare comparable to what the military developed for kinetic operations.
- Traceability, verification, and trusted digital infrastructure can help the U.S. understand the economic battlespace.
- The objective is to make compliance easier, cheaper, and more useful than evasion for a coalition of willing companies, governments, banks, insurers, and allied nations.

## SAGINT's Position in the Ecosystem

Jacob described SAGINT as a **non-custodial infrastructure provider**.

SAGINT does not:

- Mine critical minerals
- Refine critical minerals
- Touch physical assets
- Validate data directly
- Build the financial products itself
- Take a financial interest in the commodities

SAGINT does:

- Build the digital asset infrastructure
- Build the traceability system
- Receive data from validators
- Create a compliant, bankable, and insurable record of physical assets
- Integrate with validators such as ratings agencies, IoT sensors, and independent measurement bodies
- Provide the infrastructure layer that banks, insurers, assurance firms, government agencies, and industry can trust

Jacob compared SAGINT's role to **Stripe for supply chains**. The goal is to become trusted infrastructure that others build on top of.

## Technical and Legal Foundation

Jacob said SAGINT's infrastructure is built around **UCC Chapter 12** and **controllable electronic records**.

His argument is that this creates the equivalent of a digital title for a physical asset. That title can support:

- Trade finance
- Insurance
- Commodity linked notes
- Delivery insurance
- Potential ETFs
- Government enforcement action
- Sanctions enforcement
- Seizure or restriction of assets tied to sanctioned or illicit actors

Jacob emphasized that the company uses a flat pricing model rather than ad valorem rates. He described an example of **thirty cents per token**, with a token corresponding to one kilogram of neodymium.

## Standards Strategy

Jacob emphasized that SAGINT is working with **ASTM International** on a critical minerals traceability standard.

He said they intentionally did not work through ISO because of concerns that ISO standards processes are vulnerable to Chinese influence. He said Commerce trusts ASTM and that SAGINT is on the ASTM traceability council.

The objective is for the ASTM-backed traceability standard to be:

- Recognized by the U.S. government
- Adopted by industry
- Trusted by banks, insurers, assurance firms, OEMs, and government agencies
- Broad enough to become a common through-line across the market

## Strategic Vision

Jacob's three-year vision included:

- Dominating approximately 50 percent of the rare earths market
- Establishing the critical minerals traceability standard
- Getting banks, insurers, and assurance firms to use SAGINT as the underwriting through-line
- Expanding beyond critical minerals into data centers and other value chains
- Becoming the traceability system of record for the Department of War
- Extending that role across the broader U.S. government
- Scaling from critical minerals to tens of thousands of nodes across multiple value chains

## Node Strategy

Jacob defined a node as a participant in a value chain, such as a mining company, refining company, manufacturer, supplier, or other supply chain actor.

Key milestones:

- More than 50 nodes creates self-sustaining network economics
- 50 nodes could unlock more advanced financial products, such as an ETF
- The January 1, 2027 DFARS compliance deadline creates a significant adoption forcing function
- Jacob said they have mapped approximately 2,100 relevant nodes
- Initial focus areas include magnets, tantalum, and tungsten

## Funding and Scale

Jacob said SAGINT has a term sheet for a **$50 million Series A investment** and is currently raising a Series A bridge round.

The company currently has approximately 15 developers, many in the UAE, India, and elsewhere. Jacob said they are good people but not the long-term team he needs. After the funding event, he expects to replace much of the current development team with a U.S.-based technical team, ideally composed of U.S. citizens with prior clearances.

Austin, Texas is the likely hub for the technical team. Florida, especially the Palm Beach and Miami area, may serve as a finance and business hub.

## Organizational Challenge

Jacob acknowledged that SAGINT is still in startup mode and that "everyone's kind of doing everything."

The central organizational challenge is how to take raw energy and direct it toward specific objectives, especially revenue. Jacob said that after the funding event, the only thing that matters is revenue, which in this business means **nodes on the network**.

He has already thought through ten functional areas and internal tooling, but the company needs the right senior operators to build the actual organizational system and culture.
