# SAGINT

## Summary

SAGINT is a defense technology and market infrastructure company focused on traceability, compliance, and digital asset infrastructure for critical minerals and other strategically important economic value chains.

The company publicly describes itself as building critical infrastructure for the digital economy, with a focus on distributed ledger technology, cryptographic attestation, and end-to-end asset traceability.

Based on the 2026-05-28 call with [[Jacob Clayton]], SAGINT’s deeper thesis is that the United States and its allies are not competing effectively with China at the level of market infrastructure. Jacob frames SAGINT as building an **economic warfare operating system** that can make critical minerals and other strategic assets traceable, compliant, bankable, insurable, and enforceable.

## Core Thesis

SAGINT’s core thesis is that critical minerals are not just a supply chain problem. They are a market infrastructure, national security, and economic warfare problem.

The company appears to be built around several connected ideas:

1. Critical minerals and rare earths are essential to defense, energy, data centers, advanced manufacturing, and strategic competition.
    
2. China has structural advantages in mining, processing, refining, pricing, and market infrastructure.
    
3. The United States and its allies need trusted traceability infrastructure to verify provenance, compliance, ownership, and movement.
    
4. Traceability should make compliant behavior easier and cheaper than evasion.
    
5. Trusted traceability can unlock financing, insurance, commodity-linked instruments, enforcement authorities, and government adoption.
    
6. A neutral, non-custodial platform can become the trusted through-line across producers, processors, manufacturers, banks, insurers, assurance firms, and government agencies.
    

## What SAGINT Does

SAGINT is building digital asset and traceability infrastructure for physical economic value chains.

The company does not appear to position itself as a miner, refiner, validator, bank, insurer, or commodity trader. Instead, it is trying to become the infrastructure layer that allows validated information about physical assets to become useful for compliance, finance, insurance, and enforcement.

Jacob described SAGINT as similar to **Stripe for supply chains**.

Potential platform functions:

- Asset traceability
    
- Per-element traceability for rare earths and critical minerals
    
- Cryptographic attestation
    
- Digital asset infrastructure
    
- UCC Chapter 12 controllable electronic records
    
- Compliance workflows
    
- Chain of custody
    
- Integration with independent validators
    
- Support for underwriting and insurance
    
- Support for trade finance and commodity-linked products
    
- Government enforcement and economic warfare functionality
    

## Product Concept

SAGINT’s platform appears to function as a trusted coordination and traceability layer.

The platform receives information from validators and supply-chain participants, then creates a digital representation of the asset that can support compliance, financing, insurance, and enforcement.

Key concepts:

- **Non-custodial infrastructure:** SAGINT does not own or physically control the asset.
    
- **Independent validation:** Validators may include ratings agencies, sensors, assurance providers, or other trusted measurement bodies.
    
- **Controllable electronic records:** The platform uses UCC Chapter 12 concepts to create digital records that can function like title.
    
- **Bankable and insurable assets:** Once the asset record is trusted, banks and insurers can underwrite products around it.
    
- **Flat-fee token model:** Jacob described a flat fee model rather than an ad valorem pricing model.
    
- **Recursive traceability:** The system is designed for non-linear supply chains where assets move through multiple stages of transformation, separation, processing, and refinement.

## Strategic Problem

The strategic problem SAGINT is trying to solve is the lack of trusted, scalable, secure traceability infrastructure for critical supply chains.

Current issues:

- Critical minerals markets are opaque.
    
- China has significant leverage across mining, refining, processing, and pricing.
    
- Many traceability systems are top-down and expensive.
    
- Companies are reluctant to put proprietary supply chain data into open databases.
    
- Existing compliance tools may not scale across complex, non-linear supply chains.
    
- Banks and insurers need trusted data before they can underwrite products.
    
- Government needs transaction-level visibility to enforce restrictions and understand adversary supply chains.
    
- The U.S. needs market infrastructure that supports economic statecraft without destroying the market trust that gives the U.S. and allies an advantage.
    

## Economic Warfare Framing

Jacob frames SAGINT as an **economic warfare operating system**.

This framing connects directly to the CSIS article Jacob co-authored with Benjamin Jensen, which argues that modern economic warfare should focus on the adversary’s conversion function. In other words, economic warfare should target the systems that allow adversaries to convert resources, capital, logistics, and technology into military power.

For SAGINT, the relevant application is traceability.

If the United States and its allies cannot verify where critical materials come from, who handled them, and whether they are compliant, then sanctions, export controls, trusted sourcing rules, and industrial policy become difficult to enforce.

SAGINT’s strategic logic:

- Traceability enables verification.
    
- Verification enables compliance.
    
- Compliance enables finance and insurance.
    
- Finance and insurance enable market depth.
    
- Market depth enables allied alternatives to China-controlled systems.
    
- Trusted markets create economic warfare leverage.
    

## Market Infrastructure Thesis

Jacob repeatedly emphasized that the United States is not playing effectively at the **market infrastructure level**.

The argument:

- China does not only compete by controlling mines and processors.
    
- China also competes by shaping pricing, exchanges, financing, information, and market access.
    
- The United States needs trusted market infrastructure that can support allied critical mineral markets.
    
- Compliance should be embedded in the market structure rather than imposed after the fact.
    
- If SAGINT becomes the trusted traceability layer, financial institutions and government agencies may use it as a system of record.

Longer-term goals:

- Extend from critical minerals into data centers and additional value chains.
    
- Build a trusted market infrastructure layer used by banks, insurers, assurance firms, OEMs, and government.
