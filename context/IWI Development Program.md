# IWI Development Program

## BLUF

The IWI Development Program is one of my strongest nonprofit, fundraising, sponsorship, and partnership proof points. At the Irregular Warfare Initiative, I helped build the organization’s development and partnerships function from a relatively informal model into a more structured institutional sponsorship program.

This work is especially relevant to roles involving foundation leadership, community impact, partnerships, donor engagement, sponsorship strategy, and mission-aligned revenue growth.

## Core Narrative

At IWI, I helped turn a mission-driven platform into a more sustainable nonprofit organization by building sponsorship structure, partnership language, donor engagement tools, and institutional funding pathways.

The key challenge was not just raising money. It was building a development model that protected IWI’s credibility, preserved its apolitical stance, aligned sponsors with mission, and created durable support for the organization’s podcast, publication, newsletter, events, students, and research.

## Organization Context

The Irregular Warfare Initiative is a 501(c)(3) focused on irregular warfare analysis, education, and public discourse.

Core audience and platform assets:

* Podcast with approximately 10,000 downloads per episode
* Newsletter reach of approximately 10,000 subscribers
* Online publication platform
* Strong national security, academic, practitioner, and policy audience
* Mission-driven community focused on irregular warfare and strategic competition

## My Role

Director of Development and Partnerships.

Previously used the title Chief Development Officer.

Primary responsibilities included:

* Sponsorship strategy

## Strategic Problem

IWI had strong intellectual credibility, a growing audience, and a respected platform, but needed a more mature development function.

The organization needed to answer:

* How do we generate sustainable revenue without compromising independence?
* How do we offer sponsors clear value?
* How do we preserve an apolitical, mission-first posture?
* How do we convert audience reach into organizational support?
* How do we professionalize sponsorship and donor engagement?
* How do we build a development pipeline that can scale?

## Key Accomplishments

## Institutional Sponsorship Program

Built and launched IWI’s institutional sponsorship program in 2025.

This created a structured way for companies, institutions, and mission-aligned partners to support IWI while receiving clear, appropriate, and transparent sponsor benefits.

## First Multi-Year Institutional Sponsor

Secured IWI’s first multi-year institutional sponsor.

Value:

* 75,000 dollars total
* 25,000 dollars per year
* Three-year commitment

This was a major proof point that IWI could move beyond ad hoc support and build durable institutional funding.

## Corporate Sponsor Growth

Grew corporate sponsors by ten between CY24 and CY25.

This helped demonstrate that the organization could build and manage a broader sponsor portfolio.

## Budget Growth

Contributed to a 50 percent net increase in top-line budget.

This is a key proof point for future roles because it shows development work tied to organizational growth, not just relationship management.

## Public Fundraising Campaign

Led or supported public fundraising campaign efforts that helped produce a 10 times year-over-year increase in donations from CY24 to CY25.

This is an important donor engagement and campaign execution proof point.

## Sponsorship Prospectus

Created a sponsorship prospectus that translated IWI’s mission, audience, platform, and value proposition into a clear donor and sponsor-facing document.

The prospectus helped explain:

* What sponsors support

Core principle:

## Risks or Sensitivities

## Sponsor Influence

Need to avoid any perception that sponsors influence editorial judgment, research, podcast content, or institutional views.

## Political Neutrality

IWI’s apolitical stance is core to credibility. Development language must reinforce mission alignment without political advocacy.

## Overcommercialization

Sponsorship should not make IWI feel like an advertising platform. It should feel like partners are supporting serious intellectual work, education, and public discourse.

## Deliverables Management

Sponsor benefits must be tracked and fulfilled consistently. This requires a clear deliverables tracker, internal owner, timeline, and follow-up process.
